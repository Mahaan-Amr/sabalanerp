# Preserve customer request across optimizer calculations

Customer-entered longitudinal quantity, length, and width remain unchanged in sales, accounting, delivery, and workshop views. Quantity-zero optimization is an internal geometry truth for source-material consumption, canonical remaining inventory, and geometry-sensitive pricing, but it neither replaces the contract row nor dictates how the workshop physically cuts the stone. Thus `5 × 10m × 7cm` may price one long edge as `50m` and one short edge as `0.35m`, while every primary row remains the customer's `0 / 50m / 7cm`; optimizer-derived remainder is immediately allocatable without workshop approval. The production-piece breakdown is visible only to sales operators in the create/edit modal as `مبنای محاسبات داخلی`, never in customer PDFs, accounting, delivery, workshop, or other primary summaries. The informational `سنگ مصرفی` row is deliberately different: print/PDF output keeps it visible and shows the actual source width, consumed source length, source quantity, and consumed area so customers can audit the material basis without seeing an implied workshop instruction.

For the canonical quantity-zero example, the customer product row is `0 / 50m / 7cm` and `سنگ مصرفی` is one `40cm × 10m` source band totaling `4m²`. Customer output does not expose the internal `5 × 10m × 7cm` production-piece layout or remainder inventory.

Legacy derived-quantity rows reconstruct the customer request from optimizer provenance when read, including `quantity 0` and total requested length. Persistence is corrected only by an authorized explicit contract save; finalized historical contracts are never silently bulk-migrated.

Positive quantity retains the existing per-piece contract meaning: `quantity 2 / length 50m` requests two 50-meter pieces, totaling 100 meters. Only zero or empty longitudinal quantity treats the entered length as one total-length request for internal optimization.

For positive quantity, efficient source packing is automatic whenever explicit pieces fit side-by-side across the source width under the active saw-kerf policy. The customer row remains per-piece—for example `quantity 2 / length 10m / width 20cm`—while two such pieces consume one `40cm × 10m` source band rather than `40cm × 20m`. Packing changes material consumption, not customer intent. That layout has `10m` of separating cut; calibration adds another `10m` to cutting cost when enabled but never increases source length or material consumption.

Saw kerf remains a physical feasibility constraint. With `0.3cm` kerf, two finished `20cm` pieces require more than a `40cm` source width, so the optimizer consumes two `40cm × 10m` source bands and retains the resulting `19.7cm × 10m` remainders instead of claiming an impossible one-band layout. The customer-facing `سنگ مصرفی` row shows those actual source bands, total consumed area, and the active kerf note.

Packing is scoped to one contract-product row. The optimizer never silently combines separate rows because their finishing, tools, pricing, delivery timing, and remainder ownership may differ; reuse across rows continues only through the explicit selectable-remainder workflow.

Automatic side-by-side packing of multiple explicitly requested pieces does not require the physical-splitting permission. That permission remains limited to changing one explicit logical piece into several shorter physical pieces; quantity-zero optimization retains its separately approved internal derivation semantics.

Cutting keeps its existing calibration policy: the `5 × 10m` layout produces `50m` of longitudinal cutting, plus a separate `10m` calibration cut only when calibration is enabled. Sales operators see that explanation inside the internal-calculation box; customer and downstream primary rows do not expose the optimizer breakdown.
