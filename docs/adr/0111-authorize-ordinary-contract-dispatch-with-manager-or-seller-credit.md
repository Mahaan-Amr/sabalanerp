# Authorize ordinary contract dispatch with managerial approval or seller credit

The design interview on 2026-10-03 establishes two exceptions to the actual-receipt settlement gate in ADR-0110 for ordinary Sales Contracts only. Commercial finality and all other dispatch safeguards remain required. An Accountant requests managerial dispatch approval from the Accounting Contract case beside paper-customer-signature recording, with a mandatory promised-payment date. One shared cross-workspace decision duty is visible to eligible Managers and Admins; the first committed approval or rejection wins, rejection requires a reason, and authorized self-decision follows ADR-0046. Approval covers the whole Contract. A commercial revision requires renewed managerial approval while preserving the earlier decision history.

Sales settings list Sales users by name and account in a table and allow Managers and Admins to set each responsible Seller's finite credit limit. The payment-method selection adds «استفاده از اعتبار فروشنده», where the Seller chooses an amount and sees remaining credit. Seller credit represents that Seller's responsibility for dispatch before Customer payment, not a Customer receipt. Without a financial-record dispatch entitlement or managerial approval, actual receipts plus consumed Seller credit must cover the entire Contract before dispatch is enabled. Successful financial approval with matching Sepidar and invoice amounts releases all Seller credit consumed by that Contract without waiting for Customer payment. Voiding the releasing financial evidence recomputes the consumption and can create a deficit; new consumption remains blocked until credit is available. Contract cancellation or expiration releases its credit. Lowering a limit preserves existing consumption, displays zero spendable credit when over limit, and blocks further consumption. Commercial revisions reconcile credit consumption and cannot increase it beyond available credit.

Successful financial approval with matching Sepidar and invoice amounts grants dispatch authority without waiting for actual-receipt settlement; merely creating or saving a financial record does not grant this authority. This deliberately revises ADR-0110's settlement requirement while preserving its other rules. Credit is consumed atomically on successful Contract persistence, not while editing an uncommitted payment form or creation recovery draft. Concurrent Contract saves cannot consume more than available credit. Effective managerial approval or complete coverage by actual receipts plus consumed Seller credit protects the Contract from no-financial-record expiration; partial credit without complete coverage does not. Losing dispatch authority through financial-record voiding or managerial revocation blocks further loading and physical exit unless another valid entitlement exists, while plans, evidence, and completed physical exits remain preserved. Managerial revocation requires a reason and retained history.

Both managerial approval and Seller-credit consumption require an initial promised-payment date of today or a future day. The reminder is one notification on the due date, linking directly to the Contract case, only if Customer payment remains outstanding. Managerial approval reminders reach the requesting Accountant and currently authorized Managers and Admins; Seller-credit reminders reach the responsible Seller, all Accountants currently authorized to handle that Contract, and authorized Managers and Admins. The Seller does not select an Accountant. Notifications do not create follow-up duties, stop operations, or expire dispatch authority. Pending managerial requests may be withdrawn and rejected requests may be submitted anew, with history preserved. Changing the promised-payment date after managerial approval or after saving a Seller-credit Contract requires a reason and Manager approval; the old date remains effective until approval and the next notification follows the newly approved date. Rejection of a date change retains the old date and does not revoke existing dispatch authority.

Managerial approval does not itself release already consumed Seller credit. Changing the responsible Seller does not automatically transfer the guarantee: transfer requires explicit acceptance by the new Seller and enough available credit, with previous release and new consumption committed together and original guarantor history retained. Contract deactivation alone does not release Seller credit because the outstanding obligation remains. If a Contract that has never had a financial record loses its dispatch-based expiration exemption and its original deadline has passed, it expires and releases credit unless another valid exemption exists. Revocation does not restart the deadline; Contracts that have previously had a financial record never re-enter the no-financial-record expiration policy, as established in ADR-0110.

The capabilities also apply to existing active ordinary Contracts that satisfy the eligibility conditions, without rewriting historical evidence or assigning credit or managerial authority automatically.

The user confirmed the complete shared understanding on 2026-10-03. This decision supersedes ADR-0110 solely for dispatch eligibility and the dispatch-based exemption from no-financial-record expiration; other lifecycle, evidence, financial-record, reporting, recovery, and physical-operation safeguards remain applicable.

## Independent managerial decision — 2026-10-11

The owner approved a separate managerial dispatch decision for every request, including requests submitted by an Admin or Manager. Submitting never grants dispatch authority, and the requester cannot approve or reject their own managerial request. Only active users with the `MANAGER` or `ADMIN` role and permission to review the Accounting Contract can decide; broad Accounting management permissions alone do not qualify an Accountant.

The Accountant submits without a promised-payment date. Eligible independent Managers and Admins receive a notification and one shared decision duty linking to the full Accounting Contract case. The first committed decision wins. The approver determines a mandatory promised-payment date of today or a future day during approval; rejection requires a reason and no date. The requesting Accountant receives a result notification. The Contract case displays status, decision-maker identity, decision time, the approved promised-payment date, and rejection reason when applicable.

This supersedes this ADR's initial request-date requirement and self-decision allowance for managerial dispatch requests only. Existing Seller-credit, date-change, transfer, financial, commercial-finality, expiry, revocation, reminder and other dispatch rules remain in force. Historical decisions and dates are preserved; earlier approval records are not assigned invented decision times. A commercial revision still requires renewed managerial approval. Losing an approver's current review access removes their actionable task and notification access.
