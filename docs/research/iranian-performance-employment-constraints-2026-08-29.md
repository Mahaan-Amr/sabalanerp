# Iranian constraints on performance-driven employment consequences

Status: Legal research; not legal advice  
Captured: 2026-08-29  
Issue: https://github.com/Mahaan-Amr/sabalanerp/issues/341

## Question and scope

This note identifies current Iranian legal and regulatory constraints relevant to a private-sector personnel performance system: confidential evaluation, communicating a level without evaluation detail, wage reduction or withholding increases, bonuses, promotion, demotion, discipline or other employment decisions, evidence retention, and employee access or challenge rights.

It does **not** select product policy. It separates:

- **Binding**: an enacted Iranian statute, currently applicable wage determination, or ratified convention;
- **Non-binding guidance**: an issuing authority's expressly advisory interpretation;
- **Inference**: a cautious consequence drawn from binding text, requiring validation against the actual employer, contract, workplace custom, job-classification scheme, and facts;
- **Uncertain / counsel required**: the reviewed primary materials do not safely resolve the point.

The baseline assumption is a worker and employer governed by the Labour Law. That assumption must be checked person by person: Labour Law articles 1–5 establish its general scope, while article 188 excludes persons governed by the national civil-service regime or another special employment law and family workshops meeting its conditions. The result can therefore differ for public-sector personnel, specially regulated personnel, family workshops, and any workplace covered by a valid small-workshop exemption under article 191. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 1–5, 188, 191 (ILO NATLEX record linking the Persian text supplied by the Ministry of Cooperatives, Labour and Social Welfare)](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)

## Executive findings

1. Iranian labour law does not make an internal performance score a self-executing employment instrument. Existing wage, mandatory annual wage increases, job-classification obligations, major changes to customary working conditions, discipline, and termination each have their own legal source and procedure. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 8, 10, 26–27, 34–50, 157–166](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
2. No reviewed binding Labour Law provision expressly requires an employer to disclose the full internal evaluation, raw score, ranking, or supervisor narrative to a worker. Conversely, no reviewed source establishes that a level-only communication is sufficient when the employer later relies on the underlying evaluation for an adverse wage, disciplinary, demotion, or termination decision. **Uncertain / counsel required.**
3. A performance record can be evidence, but it cannot replace the legal predicates and procedure for a consequence. For article 27 termination, for example, the employer must prove neglect of assigned duties or the relevant disciplinary violation and comply with the required worker-representative/tribunal process; an adverse score alone does not satisfy those elements. **Binding as to the required predicates; inference as to the score's insufficiency.** [Labour Law, art. 27](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
4. The strongest unresolved area is personal-data law. The Electronic Commerce Law contains consent, purpose limitation, accuracy, access, correction, and qualified deletion rules for personal data messages, but its text frames those rules around electronic transactions. Whether and how articles 58–61 apply to an employer's internal ERP performance records is not safely resolved by the reviewed sources. **Binding text; uncertain scope.** [Electronic Commerce Law, arts. 2(t), 58–61 (WIPO Lex reproduction of the enacted Iranian law)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)
5. Before any production use links evaluation to pay or employment consequences, qualified Iranian labour counsel should validate the worker population, contracts, workplace custom, collective agreements, approved job-classification plan, disciplinary rules, annual wage circular, notice and defence process, and evidence-retention period. Data/privacy counsel should separately validate the lawful basis, transparency, access/correction, deletion exceptions, security, internal recipients, and any cross-border hosting or support access.

## Confidential evaluation and communicating a level without details

### What the sources establish

- The Labour Law regulates employment terms, wage, discipline, termination, and disputes, but the reviewed provisions do not prescribe a general private-sector performance-review format or a general duty to publish raw scores, rankings, or supervisor narratives. **Binding absence in the reviewed statute, not proof that no sectoral or contractual duty exists.** [Labour Law](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Electronic Commerce Law article 59 requires, where its personal-data rules apply and the data subject has consented, specified and clearly described purposes, collection limited to the stated purpose, use only for that purpose, accurate and current data, access to the relevant files, and the ability to remove or amend partial or incorrect data. It also states a qualified ability to request complete removal while observing applicable regulations. Article 58 separately requires explicit consent for listed sensitive categories, including moral characteristics and physical, psychological, or sexual condition. **Binding text; applicability to an internal employment ERP is uncertain.** [Electronic Commerce Law, arts. 58–61](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)
- Electronic Commerce Law articles 11–16 recognize secure electronic records and govern their evidential value; article 81 requires holders of data messages to maintain appropriate safe copies. These provisions support integrity and availability but do not state a performance-record retention duration. **Binding text.** [Electronic Commerce Law, arts. 11–16, 81](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)
- Civil Liability Law article 1 imposes compensation liability where an unauthorized intentional or negligent act injures another person's legally protected interests, expressly including dignity/reputation, and causes material or moral harm. This is a potential liability boundary for inaccurate or unjustifiably disclosed evaluations, not a specific employee-record confidentiality code. **Binding; application is fact-sensitive.** [Civil Liability Law, art. 1; official current-status lookup is available through the Presidency's National Laws and Regulations Portal](https://qavanin.ir/)

### Safe legal conclusions and unresolved points

- A private, self-facing level badge with a standard meaning is not expressly forbidden by the reviewed Labour Law. A level visible only to the worker and genuinely authorized employment decision-makers is easier to reconcile with purpose limitation and dignity than broad workplace publication. **Inference, not a product decision.**
- A level-only message must not be treated as legal notice or an opportunity to defend where a separate law, contract, disciplinary bylaw, collective agreement, or approved job-classification process requires reasons, warnings, consultation, or review. **Inference from the consequence-specific rules; counsel required for the applicable process.**
- Showing a worker's level to managers or supervisors who do not need it for a defined employment purpose increases purpose-limitation and reputational risk. The reviewed sources do not supply a universal role matrix or a minimum disclosure rule. **Inference / counsel required.**
- If criteria collect health, psychological, religious, moral-character, ethnicity, or other article 58 information, explicit consent and the precise statutory scope require counsel before collection. A performance criterion should not be assumed to escape article 58 merely because a supervisor supplies it. **Binding risk trigger; counsel required.** [Electronic Commerce Law, art. 58](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)

## Salary reduction and withholding increases

### Existing wage and deductions

- Employment-contract terms may not grant a worker less than statutory protections (article 8), and the contract must state the type of work/duties, wage and supplements, hours, holidays, workplace, date, duration where fixed, and other customary terms (article 10). **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 8, 10](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Labour Law articles 34–36 define remuneration, wage, fixed wage, and base wage broadly. Article 37 governs timely payment. Article 41 requires the Supreme Labour Council to set annual minimum wages, and article 42 prohibits payment below the legal minimum. Article 45 limits deductions from wages to enumerated cases. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 34–45](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Iran ratified ILO Convention No. 95, which is in force for Iran. Article 8 of the Convention permits wage deductions only under conditions and to the extent prescribed by national law/regulation, collective agreement, or arbitration award, and requires workers to be informed of those conditions. **Binding international obligation.** [ILO NORMLEX: Iran ratification status](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A11300%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3Ap11300_instrument_id%3A312240), [Convention No. 95, art. 8](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A12100%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP12100_ILO_CODE%3AC095%2C)

**Consequence:** an employer cannot safely implement a performance result as an automatic deduction from earned wage or as a unilateral reduction below legal, contractual, collective, approved-classification, or customary entitlements. Whether a proposed reduction above the minimum is lawful still depends on the contract, workplace custom, collective instruments, article 26, and the approved classification plan. **The first sentence is an inference from binding limits; the second is counsel required.**

### Mandatory annual increases versus discretionary increases

- The official 1405 wage circular applies a stated increase to “other wage levels” based on the worker's last 1404 wage, establishes minimum wage and seniority-base amounts, and governs classified workplaces. It does not make those increases contingent on a performance score. **Binding for covered workers during 1405.** [Government Information Base, “Circular determining the minimum wage for 1405,” clauses 1–5](https://dolat.ir/detail/480104)
- The same circular allows covered workplaces to establish or increase wages and benefits tied to production and productivity through workplace agreements and collective agreements with Ministry approval. **Binding for the circular's scope; it is not a general authorization to remove statutory or accrued entitlements.** [1405 wage circular, clause 7](https://dolat.ir/detail/480104)

**Consequence:** withholding the mandatory annual increase because of a low performance level would conflict with the reviewed 1405 circular. A separate, genuinely discretionary merit increase may potentially depend on performance, but counsel must first determine whether it has become contractual, customary, collectively agreed, part of an approved classification arrangement, or otherwise accrued. Annual wage instruments change, so the instrument effective for each consequence date must be evaluated. **First sentence: inference from current binding circular. Remaining sentences: uncertainty / counsel required.**

## Bonuses

- Labour Law article 34 includes production-increase bonus and annual profit participation among payments made to a worker by reason of the employment relationship. Article 47 provides for a production-increase/quality bonus through an agreement conforming to the applicable Ministry regulation. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 34, 47](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- The separate Law on Annual Eid and Bonus for Workers Covered by the Labour Law requires covered employers to pay the statutory annual amount (pro-rated for service shorter than one year), subject to its statutory floor/formula and cap. The obligation is not expressed as performance-contingent. **Binding.** [Law on Annual Eid and Bonus for Workers Covered by the Labour Law, enacted 1370/12/06; verify the current official text in the National Laws and Regulations Portal](https://qavanin.ir/)

**Consequence:** “bonus” is not one legal category. A system must not treat statutory Eid, an earned contractual or customary bonus, a production bonus under article 47, and a prospective discretionary award as interchangeable. Performance conditions, forfeiture, proration, and the point at which each amount accrues require instrument-by-instrument legal review. **Inference / counsel required.**

## Promotion and demotion

- The employment contract identifies the type of work, profession, or duty and remuneration (article 10). A major change in working conditions contrary to workplace/local custom requires prior written approval from the local labour office; disputes go to the Labour Dispute Board (article 26). **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 10, 26](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Articles 48–50 establish job-classification schemes to align job descriptions, responsibility, and base wage and require Ministry approval/enforcement for covered workplaces. The official 1405 circular also states that promotion of job grade follows instructions of the Ministry's labour-relations and compensation authority. **Binding where the workplace is covered by an approved scheme.** [Labour Law, arts. 48–50](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843), [1405 wage circular, clause 5](https://dolat.ir/detail/480104)

**Consequence:** the Labour Law does not create a general right to promotion merely from a high internal score, nor does a low score itself authorize demotion. Entitlement and authority may arise from the contract, collective agreement, workplace custom, approved classification plan, or sector-specific rules. A demotion that changes duties, grade, status, or pay may engage article 26 and the classification rules even if base pay is nominally preserved. **Inference / counsel required.**

## Discipline, termination, and other employment decisions

- Under Labour Law article 27, termination for worker misconduct requires either neglect in assigned duties or violation of workplace disciplinary rules after written warnings, plus the prescribed positive opinion/decision path involving the Islamic Labour Council, guild association, worker representative, or Recognition Board as applicable. If unresolved, the dispute proceeds through the labour-dispute bodies. The article also requires payment of accrued claims and statutory severance. **Binding.** [Labour Law, art. 27](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- The Ministry's 1402 Advisory Opinion No. 20 says expressly that it is advisory and creates no obligation. It nevertheless records the issuing labour-relations authority's view that neglect and disciplinary-rule violation are separate article 27 grounds; that the required representative opinion and disciplinary process must be followed; that the worker may complain to the Recognition Board; and that the board must itself establish the alleged neglect/violation. **Non-binding guidance.** [Ministry of Cooperatives, Labour and Social Welfare, Advisory Opinion No. 20, 1402/11/16, reproduced with its express non-binding disclaimer](https://bidbarg.net/regulation/opinion/242539/)
- Labour disputes arising from the Labour Law, employment contract, apprenticeship contract, workplace agreements, or collective agreements proceed under articles 157–166. Article 159 permits objection to a Recognition Board decision within 15 days of notification; article 165 requires reinstatement and back pay where dismissal is found unjustified. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 157–166](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Iran has ratified ILO Conventions No. 100 (equal remuneration) and No. 111 (discrimination in employment and occupation), both in force. Labour Law article 38 also requires equal wage for equal work under equal conditions in the same workplace and prohibits wage discrimination on listed grounds. **Binding.** [ILO NORMLEX: Iran ratifications](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A11200%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP11200_COUNTRY_ID%3A102800), [Labour Law, art. 38](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- Iran has not ratified ILO Convention No. 158 on termination of employment. It should not be represented as a binding Iranian obligation. **Verified treaty status.** [ILO NORMLEX: countries that have not ratified Convention No. 158](https://normlex.ilo.org/dyn/nrmlx_en/f?p=NORMLEXPUB%3A11310%3A0%3A%3ANO%3A%3AP11310_INSTRUMENT_ID%3A312303)

**Consequences:**

- A performance system may preserve evidence relevant to assigned-duty neglect, but a score, percentile, level, or manager acceptance is not the legal finding or procedure required by article 27. **Inference.**
- Criteria or consequence rules that directly or indirectly discriminate on protected grounds, or that compare unlike jobs without a defensible job-related basis, create equality risk. **Inference from binding equality rules.**
- Non-renewal of a fixed-term contract, termination, redundancy, probationary termination, discipline short of dismissal, and negotiated separation have different legal bases. They should not be collapsed into a generic “low performance consequence.” **Inference; counsel must map each intended action.**

## Employee access, correction, and challenge

- Labour Law articles 157–166 provide a forum and appeal route for covered disputes; article 27 specifically supports challenge to dismissal. The statute does not make an internal HR approval final or immune from external review. **Binding.** [Labour Law, arts. 27, 157–166](https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=21843)
- If Electronic Commerce Law article 59 applies, the data subject has access to files containing personal data messages and can remove or amend partial or incorrect data; the qualified deletion language must be reconciled with other legal duties. **Binding text; uncertain applicability and exceptions.** [Electronic Commerce Law, art. 59](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)

**Unresolved:** no reviewed primary source safely establishes that every private-sector worker has a standalone right to receive the employer's complete performance report before any consequence. However, denying all meaningful access while relying on contested facts in a wage or employment dispute may impair the worker's practical ability to challenge the action and the employer's ability to prove it. Counsel should determine the required notice, reasons, evidence disclosure, correction/response process, representative involvement, and appeal language for each consequence. **Inference / counsel required.**

## Retention and evidential integrity

- The reviewed Labour Law provisions do not state a dedicated retention period for performance evaluations. **Binding absence in the reviewed statute; sectoral rules may differ.**
- Secure electronic-record rules value integrity, attribution, date/time, accessibility, and protection against alteration; electronic evidence cannot be rejected solely because of its electronic form. **Binding.** [Electronic Commerce Law, arts. 8, 11–16](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/7711)
- Where a record supports wage, bonus, discipline, demotion, termination, or litigation, premature deletion can undermine proof and conflict with a legal hold, while indefinite retention can conflict with purpose limitation and any applicable access/deletion right. **Inference.**

**Counsel required:** determine a retention schedule by record class and trigger (draft, rejected, accepted, superseded, consequence proposal, payroll effect, disciplinary warning, termination evidence, active dispute, final judgment), including suspension of deletion during any complaint, labour-board proceeding, judicial review, audit, or reasonably anticipated claim. No single defensible number was found in the reviewed primary materials.

## Questions that require qualified Iranian counsel before reliance

1. Which Sabalan personnel are Labour Law workers, and which are governed by a special employment regime, public-sector rules, a family-workshop exclusion, or a valid article 191 exemption?
2. What contracts, collective agreements, workplace customs, approved job-classification plans, and disciplinary bylaws currently bind each employing entity and workplace?
3. Does the intended evaluation data flow fall within Electronic Commerce Law articles 58–61, and what other current Iranian privacy, cyber, sectoral, or National Data and Information Management rules apply to a private ERP operator and its hosting/support providers?
4. Is consent legally available and valid in the employment relationship for each data category, especially article 58 sensitive data, and what notice, access, correction, response, and deletion exceptions apply?
5. May a worker receive only a level summary while details remain confidential when no adverse action follows? What additional reasons and evidence must be disclosed before each wage, bonus, demotion, disciplinary, non-renewal, or termination action?
6. For every compensation component, is it statutory, mandated by the current annual wage circular, contractual, customary, collectively agreed, classification-based, accrued, production-linked under article 47, or genuinely prospective and discretionary?
7. Would a proposed duty, title, grade, reporting-line, workplace, schedule, status, or pay change be a “major change” under article 26 or require action under an approved job-classification plan?
8. What exact warnings, disciplinary-committee steps, representative opinion, notice, evidentiary standard, and labour-board steps apply before relying on performance evidence for article 27 action?
9. What retention periods and legal-hold rules apply to each record class, and which payroll, insurance, tax, corporate, sectoral, and litigation rules extend retention?
10. Do any cross-border hosting, analytics, backups, remote support, or exports create additional localization, security, transfer, or governmental-access obligations?

## Source quality and currency

Research was checked on 2026-08-29 against the Iranian Presidency's [National Laws and Regulations Portal](https://qavanin.ir/), the [National Legal Information Portal](https://dotic.ir/) operated by the Vice Presidency for Legal Affairs, the official Government publication of the [1405 wage circular](https://dolat.ir/detail/480104), the ILO's legislation and treaty-status systems, and WIPO Lex's reproduction of the enacted Electronic Commerce Law. The Labour Law NATLEX record identifies the Ministry of Cooperatives, Labour and Social Welfare as the source of its linked Persian text and identifies Official Gazette No. 13387 (1991-02) as publication metadata.

Some Iranian official legal-portal detail pages were not retrievable through the research environment. Before operational reliance, counsel should retrieve certified/current Persian texts, amendments, applicable annual circulars, classification instructions, and relevant binding decisions directly from the Official Gazette or official national portal. Pending bills and media reports were not treated as law.
